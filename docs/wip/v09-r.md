# v0.9 lane R: SuperSplat links that work, and stochastic drawing as the desktop default

Owner's items 7 and 13 (message 22; [`v09-plan.md`](v09-plan.md)): `https://superspl.at/scene/2f1ddf5f?budget=8&stochastic`
(SuperSplat's beta, desktop only) "does not work in our simulator", he cannot add such addresses; desktop pilots must
get stochastic drawing by default, and the day SuperSplat makes it the default (links lose the parameters) we must
already behave the same. Prior art: [`v07-r.md`](v07-r.md) (links, policy, engine 2.23.2), [`v08-c.md`](v08-c.md) (governor).

## Why it "did not work": every path, measured

Probe: `tools/bench/src/probe-v09-r-links.ts`, this PC (Chrome 155, the owner's 4K monitor at 30 Hz), the BASE build
(631fd5f, local release under the enforced CSP) and the LIVE site, scene 2f1ddf5f (259 M splats, 13 levels, no walls),
12 s to 30 s of settling after the scan is ready. Evidence: `evidence/2026-10-17/v09-r-probe-links-2f1ddf5f*.json`.

| Path | Base build, 1280x800 | Base build, 3840x2160 @ 30 Hz | Live site, 3840x2160 @ 30 Hz |
|---|---|---|---|
| `?scene=<whole link, percent-encoded>` (the landing) | stochastic, 8 M | stochastic, 8 M, governor step 0, 0 changes in 30 s | same |
| `?scene=<whole link pasted raw>` | stochastic, 8 M | - | same |
| `?scene=2f1ddf5f&budget=8&stochastic` | stochastic, 8 M | - | - |
| the picker's own field, link typed + Enter | stochastic, 8 M | - | same |
| control: `?scene=2f1ddf5f` | sorted, 4 M | sorted, 4 M (one streaming dip, recovered) | sorted, 4 M |

So for the owner's exact URL pasted as a SuperSplat link (or as a deep link with `&`), the parser, the picker's
field, the governor and the streamed LOD scan (engine `gsplat.stochastic` true) were NOT broken: on the same PC it
drew stochastic at 8 M. What made it "not work" for him was around that (most probable first):

0. **SuperSplat's own syntax, copied literally into OUR address, was refused.** SuperSplat says "add
   `?budget=8&stochastic` to the URL"; our address already has `?scene=...`, so the literal copy is
   `/fly/?scene=2f1ddf5f?budget=8&stochastic` (a second `?`), and the picker's field had the same trouble with
   `2f1ddf5f?budget=8&stochastic`. The base build answers both with the picker and "This is not a SuperSplat link
   or scene id" (`probe-v09-r-badforms.ts`, evidence `v09-r-badforms-base.json`): "I can't test it, I can't add
   such addresses". I cannot prove this is what he typed; it is the one path where his exact string, put the way
   SuperSplat tells you to put it, is rejected. Fixed: a scene id followed by `?` or `&` and parameters is a link
   (`link.ts`, six hand-written forms now open, `v09-r-badforms-new-after.json`).
1. **Nobody could see it.** The only sign was a HUD line for 4 s, usually behind the loading and Controls screens; F3
   said nothing about the drawing. A grain you cannot tell from a normal frame is "not working". (Fix: F3 line
   "Render: stochastic (the link) · budget 8 M splats (the link)".)
2. **The pilot's setting beat the link.** v0.7 order: Display -> Drawing (`sorted`) outranked `?stochastic`, silently.
   Anyone who had tried the setting while testing got a sorted scene from the owner's link. Base-build control D4b.
3. **Nothing was stochastic unless a link said so.** A plain link, a deep link, a picker card, N / the rotation, the
   scene pages: all sorted. He could only get stochastic by hand-writing the parameters (item 13).
4. **The admin could not take the address.** A scene already in the list stopped at "Already in the list" and lost
   the link's drawing; "sorted" could not be stored at all (checkbox, `admin.py` dropped `stochastic: false`).
5. **Scene pages and landing cards wrote the owner's render into their `/fly/?scene=` links**, which makes it a
   link parameter and so outranks the pilot's setting (it must not: owner < pilot).
6. Links as people paste them (`<...>`, quotes, a trailing full stop, the viewer's `/view?id=`) were "not a
   SuperSplat link".

## Design

**Order** (render-pc `drawing.ts` `resolveDrawing(ask, device)`, pure, each answer carries who decided it):
link (`?budget`, `?stochastic`; the page's deep link or a pasted link) > the pilot's settings (Display -> Render
`display.drawing` auto / stochastic / sorted, and Splat budget `display.budget` auto / 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 / 16 / 32 M, when
not auto) > the owner's per-scene entry (`render` in the catalogue, admin) > the device's default. The default is
stochastic for a desktop browser whose renderer runs WebGPU, at `STOCHASTIC_BUDGET_M` = 4 M (the sorted drawing's
budget and SuperSplat's viewer's desktop budget; the first cut had 8 M, see "Load cost" below), and for phones,
tablets (`isHandheld`) and WebGL: sorted at their lighter budget (2 M phones, 4 M iPadOS/desktop WebGL), nothing
said. A link or a Splat budget setting asks for more (`?budget=8` is honoured). A request a device cannot meet still names who asked.
A setting changed while flying beats the link for that scene (`app/drawing.ts setLive`, per scene object), so the
control never looks dead; Auto hands back to the link.

**Future-proof.** Only `budget` and `stochastic` are read from a link; every other parameter is ignored, never an
error. A link without them (SuperSplat making stochastic its default) asks for nothing, and nothing gets the
device default, which is stochastic on a desktop. `stochastic=0|false|no|off` stays an explicit "sorted"
(deliberate deviation from SuperSplat's `has('stochastic')`).

**Catalogue `render.stochastic` is three-valued** (true / false = "draw this scene the classic way" / absent):
`catalog.ts readRender`, `admin.py clean_scene`, `catalogueRender`. The admin editor's field is a select (Auto /
Stochastic / Sorted); pasting a listed scene's link offers "Give the scene in the list this drawing".

**Scene pages and landing cards** link `/fly/?scene=<id>` only; the simulator applies the catalogue entry itself.
The same holds for **every share path** (review, 18.10): `share/links.ts` `flightDrawing` / `cardDrawing` carry the
scene's LINK render only (`render`) and keep the owner's entry apart (`catalogue`, used to pick the scene page or
the deep link, never written into either), so `POST /api/share` and the `/s/<id>` page's fly link never carry
`&budget=4&stochastic=0` from the owner's entry (it would outrank the visitor's Render and Splat budget).

**Recording and video export.** Look (`evidence/2026-10-17/v07-render/look-*.jpg`, 1:1): stochastic leaves a dither
grain along every edge and loses fine detail a sorted frame keeps; Numbers: through the recorder's H.264 at its own
bitrate that costs 20 to 30 % more bytes and 8 to 15 dB against the clean frame. Decision:
- **Save this flight as video** (an offline render of the flight log at the video's size, nobody watching): drawn
  sorted for the run, the pilot's drawing back at the end, after a cancel and after an error (`video-export.ts`).
  Checked in the page, `accept-v03-export.ts` E1 (stochastic before, sorted during, stochastic after) and E3 (after a
  cancel); `evidence/2026-10-17/v03-export-E1-E3.json`.
- **REC / auto-record** records the screen as the pilot sees it and is NOT switched: the picture would change under his
  hands the moment he presses REC. The pilot who wants a clean file sets Display -> Render = Sorted. This is the
  owner's call (summary question): REC drawing sorted while it runs is a small change on the same hook.
- **The share picture** (L, the card of `/s/<id>`, 1200x630 JPEG): one sorted frame (`diagnostics.ts grabView(..., { sorted: true })`
  switches the engine to sorted for that frame and back). Measured (`v09-r-load.json`, 1:1 look
  `v09-r-load-share-ba7aa29d-1920x1080-*.jpg`): the stochastic grab weighs 1.3x (`2f1ddf5f`, 104 vs 77 KB) to 2.6x
  (Opole, 228 vs 87 KB) the sorted one and the grain shows in the sky and on walls; both fit the API's 400 KB at the first
  quality step, so nothing failed, the card was only rougher and bigger. `sharePicture` now returns null when no quality
  step fits (it handed back the last, too large, one). The bug report's thumbnail (480 px) keeps what the pilot saw.
- Averaging sub-frames (the "accumulated" case) is not built: little gain, several draws per frame.

**Observability.** `window.__gsfpv.drawing` (mode, budget setting, link, catalogue entry, `from`, engine state), F3's
line (`drawing-line`), the settings rows. `accept-v09-render.ts` also reads the hybrid renderer's own state and its
shader define `GSPLAT_STOCHASTIC`, not only the `gsplat.stochastic` parameter.

## Numbers

This PC, Chrome 155 WebGPU, the owner's 4K monitor at 30 Hz (every case below draws 28.6 to 30.0 Hz: the display
caps it), camera turning 60 deg/s, governor off so both modes draw the same budget, 10 s of frames after settling.
GPU ms = the engine's timestamp-query profiler, per frame (p50 / p95), mean of 2 repeats. Scenes: Opole `ba7aa29d`
(10 levels), `2f1ddf5f` (259 M splats). Evidence: `v09-r-cost-3840x2160.json`, `v09-r-cost-window.json`.

| 4K backbuffer | sorted 4 M | stochastic 4 M | sorted 8 M | stochastic 8 M |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opole `ba7aa29d` | 3.94 / 6.0 | 2.62 / 4.4 | 5.18 / 8.2 | **3.34** / 7.0 |
| `2f1ddf5f` | 5.61 / 27.7 | 3.15 / 22.7 | 5.77 / 20.9 | **4.20** / 25.7 |

At the same budget stochastic costs 25 to 45 % less GPU time at 4K (no sort). The first cut chose stochastic at 8 M
because it costs about what the old default, sorted at 4 M, did on the GPU (3.3 vs 3.9 ms and 4.2 vs 5.6 ms p50); the
shipped default is stochastic at 4 M, which costs less than the old one (2.6 vs 3.9 ms and 3.2 vs 5.6 ms) and loads the
same, see "Load cost". The governor never stepped in 40 s at 4K, 30 Hz, at 8 M stochastic or at 4 M sorted
(`v09-r-gov-4k-default.json`, `-sorted.json`: step 0, 0 changes, scale 1; the 8 M run is the harder of the two). In the 1280x800 window the two modes are within noise (one repeat; p50 3.0 to 9.8 ms): no cost
penalty either way. VRAM (window): stochastic holds the same or less (Opole 4 M 947 vs 988 MiB, 8 M 1645 vs 1740;
`2f1ddf5f` 517 vs 496 at 4 M, 941 vs 962 at 8 M); 8 M is about 1.7x the VRAM of 4 M on Opole.
Not measured: another GPU, a 60 Hz display (the p95 here, 20 to 40 ms submit-to-done on `2f1ddf5f`, is streaming and
the 30 Hz cap, not the drawing), and a fully streamed scan: in 12 of 16 runs at 4K the scan was still loading
(`sceneComplete` false) when sampled.

**Recording** (`v09-r-recording.json`, 72 frames of one camera path, 1280x720, WebCodecs H.264 at the recorder's own
bitrate 5.5 Mbit/s, 4 M): the encoder spends its bits on grain.

| scan | bytes sorted / stochastic / 4 sub-frames averaged | PSNR vs the clean sorted frame, decoded video: sorted / stochastic / averaged |
|---|---|---|
| `39e63ce9` | 968 k / 1257 k (+30 %) / 1256 k | 32.9 / 18.1 / 20.4 dB |
| Opole `ba7aa29d` | 976 k / 1175 k (+20 %) / 1134 k | 31.9 / 24.2 / 25.8 dB |

Averaging 4 jittered sub-frames per output frame removes part of the grain and little of the bytes, at 4 draws per
output frame, so it is not worth building. Decision: Design, "Recording and video export".

## Load cost of the default budget (review, 18.10)

The GPU numbers above were all the first cut measured; the review asked about loading, the owner's top item. Bench
`v09-r-load.ts`, every run in a fresh browser context (cold HTTP cache), stochastic, governor off, the link carries the
budget, window 1280x800; evidence `v09-r-load.json`. Bytes = response bodies of everything the page fetched.

| scene | budget | bytes | drawn | ready (usable) | complete (levels in place) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opole `ba7aa29d` | 4 M | 300 MB | 3.9 M | 5.5 s | 26 s |
| | 8 M | 502 MB (+67 %) | 7.8 M | 2.6 / 2.6 s | 25 / 22 s |
| `2f1ddf5f` | 4 M | 98 MB | 3.9 M | 9.5 / 6.2 s | 25 / 13 s |
| | 8 M | 162 MB (+65 %) | 7.9 M | 6.5 / 6.6 s | 20 / 15 s |

What it shows: the page is usable at the same moment at 4 M and 8 M (ready does not wait for the budget), the bytes
rise 65 to 67 % (the same chunks, twice as many levels) and the VRAM about 1.7x (Opole 947 vs 1645 MiB, window cost
bench). Time to complete is NOT a clean signal here: the CDN, not the page, set it (two-run spread 12 to 26 s at the same
budget), and one run reported `sceneComplete` at 1.9 s with 0.9 M drawn (the engine says complete before the first
level request is out: that row's time is meaningless, its bytes show it). The earlier cost runs (`v09-r-cost-*.json`,
warm cache) had 8 M take 25 to 60 % longer to the first `sceneComplete`; on a slow link bytes decide, and 8 M is 65 %
more of them. Decision: the default is 4 M (`STOCHASTIC_BUDGET_M`, `drawing.ts`, test `drawing.test.ts`). No gain from 8 M
was ever shown, SuperSplat's viewer draws 4 M on a desktop by default (so a link that loses `?budget=8&stochastic`
behaves like a bare link), and 4 M streams and holds what the old default did. Not measured: a slow link, a GPU with
little VRAM, whether 8 M looks better at 4K (nobody compared the two looks; the owner may want it, question below).

## Owner questions

1. Default budget of the stochastic drawing on a desktop: 4 M (shipped: the old streaming and VRAM, cheaper GPU, SuperSplat's own default) or 8 M
   (SuperSplat's example link; 65 % more bytes and ~1.7x VRAM, nobody has shown it looks better)? One constant, `STOCHASTIC_BUDGET_M`, and a link or
   the Splat budget setting already gives 8 M.
2. A pilot who set Display -> Render to Sorted and opens any address with `&stochastic` (the owner's links in groups, say) gets stochastic again on
   every reload, because the link beats the stored setting and the address bar keeps the link; the change made in flight is not kept per scene.
   Keep (links always work) or let an explicit stored Sorted beat links, or keep the in-flight choice per scene? Likewise a link can ask 32 M
   from a pilot who set 2 M (the governor steps it down).
3. REC / auto-record draws as the pilot sees it, so a stochastic desktop records grain (20 to 30 % larger files). Draw sorted while REC runs?
