## 1. Research: how PC games let a player fly (read 2026-10-17)

| Game / tool | Move | Up / down | Look | Speed | Stops when keys are let go | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Minecraft (Java), creative flight | W A S D relative to the view | Space up, Shift down (double-tap Space to start flying) | mouse, always captured | Ctrl (sprint) | yes: no inertia to speak of | [Minecraft Wiki, Controls](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Controls) |
| Fortnite Creative, flying | W A S D relative to the view | Space (hold) up, left Ctrl down (double-tap Space to start) | mouse | C cycles flight speed 0.1x..10x | yes | [Epic, Creative hotkeys](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/fortnite/hotkey-and-keybinding-shortcuts-in-fortnite-creative) |
| Unity / Unreal editor, fly-through camera | W A S D relative to the view | Q down, E up | mouse while the right button is held | Shift faster, wheel sets the speed | yes | [Unity, Scene view navigation](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/SceneViewNavigation.html) |
| GTA Online drone (as ported by a PC mod; the game's own PC table was not found) | W A S D | Shift up, Ctrl down | mouse | Space boost | yes | [GTA5-Mods, GTAO Nano Drone](https://gta5-mods.com/scripts/gtao-nano-drone) |
| Browser FPV demos | W A S D | Space / Ctrl | mouse | wheel | not stated | [itch.io, FPV drone simulator prototype](https://knight-of-gamedevalry.itch.io/fpv-drone-simulator-prototype-demo) |

Roblox has no official fly scheme (the hits were exploit scripts), so it is not cited. What the web platform gives us:

- Pointer lock ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Pointer_Lock_API)): `requestPointerLock()` from a
  click; `movementX/Y` keep reporting while the cursor is held; Esc releases it, and a new engagement gesture (a click)
  is needed before the next request; `unadjustedMovement: true` turns the OS mouse acceleration off (fails with
  `NotSupportedError` on some platforms, then a plain request).
- Fullscreen ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Fullscreen_API)): `requestFullscreen()` returns a
  promise; Esc and F11 leave it; `fullscreenchange` reports the change.
- Keyboard Lock ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Keyboard_API)) can hold Escape in fullscreen, but
  it is experimental and Chromium only, and it does not give back Ctrl+W: **Ctrl is never used for flying**.

**The common denominator.** (1) The mouse is captured and looks; the view, not the body, is what the player aims.
(2) W A S D move relative to where the view faces. (3) Space goes up, Shift (or Ctrl) goes down: a vertical axis
on two keys, never a throttle that stays. (4) Let go and the player stops at once, in place: nobody "arms" or "trims".
(5) One key for faster, and flight starts with the first key, not with a switch. (6) Esc frees the mouse and pauses,
a click gets it back. The v0.8 scheme broke (1), (3) and (4): keys tilted the drone (it then drifted on, a drone
coasts), turning was on keys, Space was an arm key, Shift was only a boost.

## 2. Design

**The game scheme replaces v0.8's keyboard layout** under `input.play` = `easy` (the default; its touch half is
unchanged). `pilot` keeps Mode 2. Radios, gamepads, HID and touch are not touched; sim-core is not touched (A6).

Keys (`KeyboardEvent.code`, so any layout): W/S or Up/Down forward/back; A/D strafe; Left/Right arrows or Q/E turn (for
players without a mouse); Space up; Shift down (hold on the ground to land); F full screen; Shift+F next favourite;
Esc/P pause; K the key card. Ctrl is never read. A flying key starts the motors by itself (Shift alone does not).

**Assist layer** (`apps/fly/src/play/game.ts`, pure, no DOM, the caller passes time and the model's state). Intent in,
sticks out, for the existing angle-mode physics; the log records sticks, a replay never runs the assist (as assist.ts).
- Horizontal: a velocity target in the world frame along the **aim** heading (forward/right components from keys,
  normalised so a diagonal is not faster), ramped (accelerate 6 m/s^2, brake 9 m/s^2), then a PI velocity loop ->
  wanted acceleration -> tilt angle (`atan(a/g)`, capped at the smaller of 55 deg and the drone's angle limit) in the
  body's own heading frame -> stick through the drone's own rates curve (sampled and inverted, `StickCurve`). Keys
  released: target 0, the loop brakes and holds the place. The drone tilts as physics says (the body is never faked).
  Measured on the real model (`tools/bench/test/app-game.test.ts`): 8 m/s in about 1.8 s, back under 0.5 m/s 1.0-1.7 s
  after the release, height within 0.6 m on every preset. A drone flies as fast as tilt and drag let it: at "fast"
  (14 m/s) a Pavo20 reaches about 9.7 m/s.
- Vertical: `AltitudeHelp` (already proven in v0.8; now with `follow()` for a speed target and `holdAt()` for the
  take-off hop) with Space up, Shift down, slow/normal/fast 3/5/8 m/s, and the height hold when released. The sink speed
  is limited by the height above the ground below (`play/ground.ts`, one sweep of the scan's own collision, 20 m) and
  aimed at a touch-down of min(0.4 m/s, 0.3 x the model's bounce speed `v_bounce_ms`) after a gentle deceleration. The
  aim is the bounce speed, not the crash speed (the first build aimed at 0.2 x `v_crash_ms`): the body is a few
  spheres, the first one to touch turns it and the others come down up to 2.2 times faster than the drone does.
  Landed = Shift held, the lowest point within 15 cm of the ground, no net sink (the vertical speed averaged over
  about 0.3 s within 0.15 m/s), all for 0.4 s; the motors then stop at the bottom of a hop (never in the air above it:
  a fall from the 4 cm top of the Mach R5's hop touched at 1.8 m/s) and the next key starts them; a take-off from the
  ground hops to 1 m first. An instant speed bound is not used: a quad on idle thrust hops on the floor at +/-0.45 m/s
  and never sits under it. Measured (`app-game.test.ts`): every preset, slow / normal / fast, from 1.5, 3, 8 and 20 m,
  108 landings on a flat floor, all stop the motors within 9 s, none crashes, no sphere touches above 0.69 x
  `v_bounce_ms`; on the real scan the hardest touch is 0.6 m/s (the first build: 1.07).
- Yaw: an **aim heading** moved by the mouse (degrees per pixel x sensitivity) and by the turn keys (arrows, Q/E); a P
  loop on the wrapped error between the aim and the body's heading gives a yaw rate, turned into a stick by the rates
  curve. The body follows a 90 deg/s sweep with a lag under 6 degrees (about 55 ms), a 90 degree flick to within 4 degrees in
  0.2 s and a 180 degree flick in 0.35 s, overshooting by up to 3 degrees on the presets tried (the tests bound 0.25 s,
  0.4 s and 4 degrees; a yaw gain four times higher overshoots 7-10 degrees, the control).
  The velocity target follows the aim even while the body is still turning to it.
- Camera: the mouse's vertical movement is a **gimbal pitch**, -60..+30 deg from the horizon (clamped), invert-Y a
  setting. The camera looks along the aim at that pitch with no roll (a stabilised gimbal, `play/gimbal.ts`,
  `session.cameraAim`); the body still pitches and rolls as the physics says. Render-only, like the camera uptilt: the
  flight model never sees it. A video exported from the flight log shows the drone's own camera (the log holds channels,
  not the mouse): a known limit, noted for lane A, who owns the export.
- Mode: the scheme flies angle mode (`Controls.gameKeys()`; a levelled tilt is what the velocity loop commands). The mode
  chip shows Angle and M or a picked mode says so; `pilot` is the way to acro. The pilot school is for touch only.

**Settings** (`packages/prefs/src/defs/play.ts`, no schema bump): `input.mouseSensitivity` (0.25..3, x, default 1 =
0.1 deg per pixel), `input.mouseInvertY` (off), `input.gameSpeed` (slow / normal / fast).

**Page**: `play/mouse-look.ts` owns pointer lock (a click on the flight view locks; `pointerlockchange` out while
flying = pause menu; a click on the paused view or Continue = resume and lock; a failed request leaves the card up).
`play/fullscreen.ts` owns F and the top-bar button (`fullscreen.toggle`; works with pointer lock). The key card is the
game card: "Click to fly" and the six lines of the brief; K shows it, it is up on the ground until the first click.
