## Short answer

Yes, it is ready and it works: `playcanvas/supersplat-viewer` (MIT) has a complete WebGPU stochastic renderer with temporal accumulation, written against
`playcanvas ^2.23.0`. It is in the viewer since 2026-09-26 (PR #314, after the 1.35.0 we carry as a dev dependency, which does not contain it); upstream
calls it experimental. It takes the engine's LOD, streaming and work buffer as they are and owns everything after: projection of the resident splats in a
compute pass, a counting depth order, an unsorted opaque raster (each fragment kept with probability alpha, unbiased), a temporal resolve with
reprojection, and a compose quad that blends the result over the camera target and writes depth. The file set is self-contained: one import leaves the
renderer folder (`../picker`, two 10-line camera-snapshot helpers). Plan: copy 14 files unchanged at one commit, write 2 small replacements of what we do
not take, 7 one-line type edits, and one adapter in `render-pc`. Writing our own would be months; this is days. The open question is not "can we build it"
but "is it good for a camera that never rests" (see "Risks for an FPV camera" and "Measurement plan"): upstream never measured its picture in motion
(v10-r section 4), so that is the first thing this lane measures, with the engine's stochastic as the base and sorted as the reference.

## How the pieces work together (one frame)

1. The engine runs its gsplat update for the camera and layer: LOD choice under `splatBudget`, streaming, the block allocator, the work-buffer copy (and
   its colour refresh when the camera turns past `colorUpdateAngle`). Inside `GSplatManager.update()` (engine 2.23.2: `world.bake` -> the engine's own
   `renderer.prepareRenderView` -> `frame:ready`) the provider's `frame:ready` listener fires. The provider has replaced the engine renderer's
   `prepareRenderView` by `() => false` at `material:created`, so the engine's own sorter/projector/draw never run.
2. `StochasticSplatRenderer.frame(set, changed, manager)` runs in that hook: rebuild the chunk table when the resident set changed (one entry per 256
   splats of every resident node), cull nodes on the cpu (sphere against frustum, one bit each), pick the view size (backbuffer, or an offscreen camera
   target), decide `moved` (any element of the view or projection matrix changed by more than 1e-6) and `taaOn`, then per view: dispatch the projector
   (`device.computeDispatch`), the args pass, the order scan, the order scatter; bind the passes' parameters for the render graph.
3. The render graph then runs the camera's passes: `camera.beforePasses` holds, in order, the bind pass, the raster pass (into the view's own colour +
   depth target), the taa pass (history[read] + raster -> history[write]); the main forward pass of the camera draws the compose quad.
4. The compose reads history[write], applies the engine's tone map/gamma, blends over what the camera target holds (clear colour, skybox, opaque meshes) and
   writes depth, so everything drawn later (our debris/craft meshes are drawn earlier, the voxel overlay clears depth in its own layer) tests against the
   splats. The next frame swaps the history.

What it needs from the viewer around it: nothing but the `app`, the camera component, the World layer and an options bag (`source`, `mode`, `variant`,
`requestFrame`). The viewer's `quality.ts` (budget fitting by `StochasticSplatRenderer.cacheFits`), `state.stochastic` and the `stochastic:changed` event,
`FrameGate` (`requestFrame`), `FrameDepthPicker` (reads `depthFrame`, `frameDepthTexture`), the debug panel and the XR menu are consumers of it, not
dependencies. The `app` must have been created with the gsplat component system; nothing is read from `Global`, `Settings` or `State`.

## TAA parameters (defaults of `defaultVariant()`; `?variant=key:value` seeds them)

| what | value / behaviour (`shaders/taa.ts`, `stochastic-splat-renderer.ts`) |
|---|---|
| history length at rest | running mean with weight 1/count, count up to **`taaMax` 256** (field holds at most 511); the exact texel is used, no reprojection, so a resting camera converges without blur. A rest frame also keeps `frameWanted` until `restFrames` reaches `taaMax + 2` (upstream asks the app for more frames; we render every frame) |
| history length moving | cap **`taaMoveMax` 16**, then shortened by image motion: `cap = max(2, cap / (1 + speed / taaMotion))`, `taaMotion` 4 px, `speed = min(motion, parallax)` with `taaParallax` on, i.e. a pure rotation (no parallax) keeps the full 16; a history an occluder left behind (nearer than the sample) is cut by `ghost = parallax * max(prevDepth - histDepth, 0) / histDepth` over `TAA_GHOST_PX` 0.25 |
| what counts as moving | `viewMoved`: any element of the view or projection matrix differs from the previous frame by more than 1e-6; or the resident set changed this frame (detail streamed in: the history is kept, the weight capped to `taaMoveMax`). `restFrames` resets on either |
| reprojection | none at rest. Moving: the pixel's world point from its own sample depth (`taaReproj: sample`; `history` = through the pixel's accumulated mean depth) goes through the previous view-projection; a pixel with no sample reprojects through the mean depth its history remembers, so coverage fades instead of smearing. Built from the view-space point and the projection's x/y terms (not the inverse view-projection: 32-bit float error at a 1e-4 near plane shook the history) |
| history fetch moving | Catmull-Rom bicubic (16 loads; 5 bilinear taps with the half-float history, used only by XR eyes) |
| the current sample | at rest the pixel's own sample; moving the mean of its 2x2 quad (bilinear between quad centres: 4 differently thresholded samples a pixel, "a little sharpness"), mixed back toward the pixel's own by `count / (count + taaSharp)`, `taaSharp` 16 |
| ghosting / disocclusion | colour clamp of the fetched history to the 3x3 neighbourhood of this frame's samples: mean +- `taaClip` 1.25 standard deviations (moving only; 0 disables, the mobile mode); the motion/ghost cap above; history rejected when the reprojected point is off screen or its count is 0 |
| reset | size change (`resizeHistory`), the first frame, `setVariant`/`applyVariant`, `setWarp`, a history-format switch; NOT a camera move, NOT a resident-set change (capped instead). A capture at another size (a render target) does not accumulate (`allowQueryTaa` is the bench switch) |
| jitter | **none**: the camera is never jittered. The noise differs from frame to frame because the per-splat hash mixes `frameSeed = frameIndex` while taa is on, so a resting camera's average converges to the expected image without sub-pixel AA |
| first sample of an empty pixel at rest | enters with `count = restFrames` so a rare hit does not flash to full coverage |
| display | optional unsharp mask at display (`taaSharpen`, default 0), faded out by count |
| occlusion cull | `cull: auto`: previous frame's depth grid (8 px, 32 px), switched off by itself for 60 frames when a frame culled under 8 % of its splats; counts read back asynchronously |
| memory | history 12 B/px (x2 ping-pong = 24), raster colour RGBA8 + depth 32F; upstream's model: 75 MB per million of budget + 40 B/px |

## How SuperSplat decides to use it

It does not decide: it is an opt-in. The hosted viewer and the package expose a Stochastic toggle (state `stochastic`, remembered in `localStorage`, off by
default); the `stochastic` option/flag forces it on (`true`: the per-pixel mode; `'mobile'`: the interleaved-pixel-set mode for phones and headsets) or
`'off'` forbids it. `Quality.stochasticAllowed` = WebGPU and not a standalone headset (there it ties with sorted at twice the memory). The mode follows the
device ladder (`deviceClass.ladder` desktop -> `pixel`, mobile -> `mobile`). The renderer is made the first time it is wanted, switched in and out in place
(`setEnabled`), and the budget is fitted so that BOTH renderers' caches fit one storage binding, so switching never changes the budget. This matches our
D75: stochastic is one choice away, never a silent default.

## Risks for an FPV camera that is always moving fast

1. **The premise is unmeasured in motion.** Upstream's quality number (35-37 dB, v10-r section 4) is a settled picture of a resting camera after 10-20 s. In
   flight the history is at most 16 frames, usually 2-4 (see 2). Expect the moving picture to be noisier than sorted by a clear margin; the question is
   by how much against the engine's stochastic (16-23 dB, +24..61 % flicker) and against sorted. Measured first, decided by the owner after.
2. **Fast flight shortens the history to almost nothing.** At the owner's 115 deg field and 1280 px, one degree is 7 px at the centre and 11 px on average
   across the field; a flip at 500 deg/s is 60 to 90 px per frame at 60 Hz. A pure rotation keeps the 16-frame cap (no parallax), but a near wall at 6-10 m/s gives tens of pixels of parallax a frame:
   `cap = max(2, 16 / (1 + speed / 4))` is 2 above ~28 px. So in a room the picture is the quad-mean of one frame plus a short history: about the
   single-frame figure with the quad's blur. That is the honest worst case.
3. **Ghosting and smearing.** Reprojection is through one layer's depth per pixel while a stochastic pixel is a mixture of layers; the 3x3 clamp (1.25
   sigma), the parallax cap and the nearer-history rule fight it. Under a flip or a gate pass, edges can trail. Measured as a lag index and a ghost-pixel
   count (below). If it shows, the knobs are `taaMoveMax`, `taaMotion`, `taaClip`, `taaReproj`.
4. **Latency.** No accumulation latency in the stick-to-screen sense: the frame shown is this frame's sample folded into the reprojected history; nothing is
   held back. What lags is the colour change (a surface that changes shade, a LOD pop) which blends in over up to `cap` frames. The history is counted in
   frames, not milliseconds: at the owner's 30 Hz monitor a frame is 33 ms, so 16 frames are half a second of blend, twice what 60 Hz gives; at 30 Hz the
   motion per frame is also twice as large and the cap shortens by itself. Measure at 30 Hz-equivalent paths (double the step per frame).
5. **Resets on resolution changes.** The governor changes the render scale in steps; each change resizes the history (reset, one frame). Rare and
   already hysteretic, but a bad governor episode would flash.
6. **Cost.** More passes (project, args, order scan, order scatter, raster, taa, compose) and memory at 4K: history 24 B/px + targets 16 B/px = 40 B/px,
   about 330 MB at 3840x2160, plus 75-125 MB per million splats (the work-buffer source keeps the engine's work buffer, so the upper end). The owner's
   monitor is 4K at 30 Hz. Measured at 1280x720 and 4K against sorted.
7. **First-use hitches.** Projector variants compile lazily (occlusion/plain, per coverage mode) and `cull: auto` flips between them; the first flight
   frame after enabling may hitch. Pre-warm in the adapter if the bench shows it.
8. **Engine coupling.** Internal classes (`gsplatDirector`, `GSplatManager.world`, indirect draw slots) of a version we pin. A bump of the engine needs the
   Stage 3 controls run again; the adapter's feature check turns a moved internal into the fallback instead of a crash. Upstream itself targets 2.23.3
   and an engine branch (`feat/webgpu-xr-depth`) for XR only; we never run XR.
9. **Upstream churn.** The renderer is days old and "experimental". The pin is a sha, the copy is byte-identical but for marked lines, and `UPSTREAM.md`
   holds the update procedure. We do not follow upstream automatically.
10. **Scene changes in one page.** `unloadSplat` destroys the entity and its assets; the engine may destroy and recreate the gsplat manager. The provider
    re-takes it on `material:created`, and `frame()` handles `this.set !== set`. Verified by a scene switch (N) with the renderer on, in the correctness checks.
11. **`ready`/`sceneComplete` after the takeover.** Our video export and loader read the engine's `frame:ready` `ready/loading`; upstream's own loader reads
    the same under the takeover and works, but ours is checked (the export runs sorted, so it only matters for the first-view reveal).

## How motion will be handled

1. Start from upstream's defaults unchanged, so the first run is "what SuperSplat shows" in our page and on our scenes. Nothing is tuned by taste.
2. In flight the camera pose changes every frame, so the moving path (cap 16, reprojection, clamp, quad mean) is the normal path, and the resting path
   (256 frames, exact texel) is what a paused or hovering-without-input drone gets. Rest is detected exactly as upstream does (matrices equal within
   1e-6), which also covers the sim being paused; no extra rule.
3. Tuning is a grid over `taaMoveMax` (8, 16, 24, 32), `taaMotion` (2, 4, 8), `taaClip` (1.0, 1.25, 1.5), `taaSharp` (8, 16, 32) on three recorded-style
   motion paths per scene, scored by the metrics below; the winner must beat the defaults on moving PSNR without a worse ghost lag index, or the defaults
   stay. Sorted-vs-accumulating is NOT a tuning target: sorted is the reference and always wins on fidelity; the owner's question is whether the accumulating
   picture is good enough, which the numbers (and his eyes on the 4K monitor) answer.
4. If the knobs cannot make the moving picture good, the result is reported as such and sorted stays the default (D75 already says so); the code is then
   an opt-in the owner can still switch on, with the engine's stochastic kept as the fallback implementation.
5. Never change the policy order, the presets or the default drawing in this lane. Whether a device class should default to the new drawing is the
   owner's decision after reading the numbers.

Bars, fixed here before any run from v10-r's figures (the owner may change them; a miss is reported, never moved):
- Q1 at 256 frames: at least 33 dB on every scene (the hosted SuperSplat viewer settled at 34.9-37.3 on three of them) and at least 10 dB above E.
- Q2: A's mean PSNR at least 4 dB above E's, A's flicker excess over S at most half of E's (E: +24..+61 %).
- Q4: lag index not negative on any frame after frame 2 after the stop; ghost pixels after the stop not above S's-vs-S's noise floor plus 1 %.
- C1: A's p95 GPU time at 4K not above S's + 25 % (proposal; the owner's 30 Hz 4K screen has a 33 ms frame).
- K1: within +-1.5 levels.
Anything that decides "make it a default somewhere" is the owner's call from these tables, not a bar of ours.

Negative controls (each must fail where it should, or the check proves nothing):
1. the harness's S vs S (two sessions, same poses): about 99 dB and equal flicker;
2. E must reproduce `v10-r-quality.json` (above);
3. A0 (`taa:off`) must read far below A at 256 frames and in Q2 (it is single-frame: 15-23 dB expected);
4. K1 with the compose's output transform deliberately skipped (a build flag in the bench only) must break the colour check;
5. Q4 with `taaClip:0, taaMoveMax:64, taaMotion:0` (no clamp, long moving history) must show a worse lag index and more ghost pixels than the defaults;
6. the adapter's fallback: a build where the feature check is forced to fail must run the engine's stochastic (v0.10 numbers) and report `impl: engine`;
7. unit tests of the pure policy (impl choice, fallbacks) fail against a policy that ignores the feature check.
