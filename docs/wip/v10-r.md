## Findings: why stochastic looks bad (A)

Tools: `tools/bench/src/v10-r-quality.ts`, `v10-r-dither.ts`, `v10-r-budget.ts`, `v10-r-supersplat.ts` (this PC, Chrome 155 WebGPU, NVIDIA
"lovelace", 1280x720 at 1:1, the owner's 4K monitor not used; every figure is PSNR / SSIM on luma against the SORTED picture of the same
view, SSIM = mean over 8x8 windows every 4 px). Pictures at 1:1: `evidence/2026-10-20/v10-r-quality/`, `v10-r-dither/`, `v10-r-supersplat/`.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Opole (outdoor, streamed) | 3.9 M | 21.8 dB / 0.52 | 28.8 dB | 22.9 dB / 0.53 | 12.5 -> 20.1 |
| Lublin 259 M (outdoor, streamed) | 3.9 M | 18.3 dB / 0.64 | 23.7 dB | 18.2 dB / 0.64 | 14.6 -> 21.7 |

(4 M budget; the 8 M rows are in `v10-r-quality.json`: the three interiors that fit under 4 M give the same picture, the streamed ones
differ by under 0.5 dB.) A picture within 30 dB of the reference is clean, 20 dB is a different picture. All five scans are in the bad
range, the interiors worst (17 to 20 dB, SSIM 0.33 to 0.50); in motion every one is worse (16 to 23 dB) and flickers 40 to 70 % more than
the sorted one. Switching the engine's temporal jitter on (a new noise pattern every frame, `camera.jitter = 1`) does not help: the single
frame is the same (+-0.1 dB) and the flicker doubles (14.6 -> 30.2 on the room).

### 2. Why: two causes, both in the engine's stochastic drawing (PlayCanvas 2.23.2), both measured

**Cause 1, one noisy sample per pixel and nothing to average it.** In the engine's stochastic drawing every splat is kept or thrown away
per pixel by a dither test against its opacity, drawn opaque with depth write, no sorting. A pixel gets ONE sample per frame. The engine's
own documentation of the setting says the dither "looks best under temporal anti-aliasing" (`gsplat-params.js` `dither`): the picture is
meant to be averaged over frames. This page does not do that (no TAA, `camera.jitter` 0: the noise pattern is frozen on the screen and
the scene slides under it, "dirty glass"). A flying camera never rests, so there is nothing to average anyway. SuperSplat's hosted viewer
does something else with `?stochastic`: its bundle has its OWN splat renderer ("StochasticSplatRenderer") with a TAA history (`taa: "on"`,
`taaMax: 256` frames while the camera rests, `taaMoveMax: 16` while it moves, cubic filter, reprojection). Same word, a different
picture: measured below (section 4). Our app uses the engine's raw one.

**Cause 2, the engine's dither makes the picture denser than alpha blending, so averaging does not reach the sorted picture.** The mean of
64 jittered stochastic frames should converge to the sorted picture if the drawing were an unbiased estimate of alpha blending. It does
not: 17.6 to 28.8 dB (table above), the wallpaper close-up not at all. The shader chunk `opacityDitherPS` keeps a fragment when
`alpha >= pow(noise, 2.2)` ("convert the noise to linear space"), so a splat of opacity a survives with probability a^(1/2.2), always more
than a (0.3 -> 0.58), and survivors are drawn OPAQUE with depth write. Where many translucent splats overlap, the front ones win far more
often than alpha blending lets them (three layers of 0.3: 34 % of the pixel left for what is behind in blending, 7 % here), so what lies
behind them (fine lines, small detail, the surface under a haze) is hidden, and the front layer's colour dominates. A small room, scanned
close, is exactly that: surfaces close to the camera built of many large overlapping semi-transparent splats, fine texture behind them.
Test (`v10-r-dither.ts`, the engine's own supported override `Material.getShaderChunks`, only the noise line of that chunk changed):

| scene | the engine's dither: 1 frame / mean of 64 frames (dB) | mean colour shift vs sorted (R, G, B levels) | without the pow 2.2: 1 frame / mean of 64 | colour shift |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| room, wallpaper close-up (interior) | 16.7 / 19.3 | +25.1, +19.0, +12.4 | 14.8 / **31.9** | -2.3, -1.6, -0.5 |
| Modlinek Villa room (interior) | 20.6 / 25.7 | -0.8, -1.3, -1.2 | 22.3 / **37.6** | +0.2, +0.1, +0.3 |
| winter garden (interior) | 16.3 / 21.6 | -0.7, 0.0, -0.8 | 17.6 / **31.0** | -0.6, -0.6, -0.4 |
| Opole (outdoor, streamed) | 22.7 / 28.8 | +1.2, +1.2, +1.4 | 22.9 / **36.5** | -0.4, -0.4, -0.1 |

Without the pow the mean of 64 frames sits 31 to 38 dB from the sorted picture and the colours match: the bias is gone. The look
(`v10-r-dither/224aba38-*-crop.png`, the same 320 x 180 piece of the wallpaper): sorted = crisp print and the fine grid; the engine's
mean of 64 frames = soft, orange, the grid gone; the unbiased mean = the sorted picture again; the engine's single frame = speckle.
(A third variant in the first run, a hash noise in place of the blue-noise texture, did not draw anything useful, its frames did not change with the jitter: its entries in `v10-r-dither.json` (`hash`) say nothing and the tool no longer has it.)
What it does NOT fix: a single frame stays noisy (14.8 to 23 dB). Making stochastic drawing good needs BOTH an unbiased dither and
temporal accumulation (what SuperSplat's own renderer has). That is a renderer feature, not a setting; not built here (see "Not done").

### 3. Is it only interiors? (the owner: "why so bad in a small room")

Interiors are the worst case, outdoor scans are bad too (18 to 22 dB): the single-frame noise is the same everywhere, the bias grows with
the number of overlapping translucent layers per pixel, which is what a close, textured, scanned surface has. The wallpaper close-up is the
extreme (colour shift of +25 levels, detail gone). So "stochastic is fine outdoors and bad in rooms" is not what the numbers say; they say
stochastic as the engine draws it is not good quality anywhere, and worst where the scan is dense and close.

### 5. What a bigger splat budget buys (the owner: "keep 4 M or raise to 8 M, +65 % traffic")

`v10-r-budget.ts`: sorted, a cold browser context per budget (bytes = every response body), four views, each against the 16 M picture of the
same view. The scan must be bigger than the budget for the budget to matter.

| scene | 2 M | 4 M | 8 M | 16 M (reference) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| winter garden (interior, big scan): bytes, picture vs 16 M | 131 MB, 28.7 dB | 222 MB, 33.4 dB / 0.96 | 366 MB (+65 %), 39.2 dB / 0.99 | drawn 15.4 M |
| Opole: bytes, picture vs 16 M | 238 MB, 29.9 dB | 411 MB, 33.2 dB / 0.93 | 703 MB (+71 %), 38.7 dB / 0.98 | 999 MB |
| Lublin 259 M: bytes, picture vs 16 M | 109 MB, 24.1 dB | 177 MB, 26.3 dB / 0.87 | 289 MB (+64 %), 29.5 dB / 0.93 | 522 MB |
| GPU time per frame (sorted, 1280x720, p50) Opole / Lublin | 0.59 / 1.5 ms | 0.85 / 2.1 ms | 1.18 / 3.6 ms | 2.1 / 5.6 ms |

The two small scans (3.8 M and 4.6 M splats in total, the first interior and the wallpaper close-up) draw the same at every budget: no
gain, no cost. On a big scan 8 M buys +5.5 to +6 dB against 16 M (the step from "visibly softer" to "hard to tell") for +64 to +71 % bytes;
nobody has said the extra bytes are free for a pilot on a slow link, and the page is usable at the same moment either way (v0.9 "Load
cost"). Decision (and the owner's to change with one field): the strong desktop's built-in budget stays 4 M, SuperSplat's own desktop
budget and the v0.9 shipped value; 8 M is one number in the admin's Drawing tab. (The server of this bench served `dist` while `dist` was rebuilt in place, base commit to this lane's: every run asked
`stochastic=0` and an explicit `budget=`, which both builds honour the same way.)

### 6. The rule (what the numbers back)

1. **Sorted is the default on every class of device, for every scene.** It is the only drawing that is within noise of itself from frame to
   frame and the only one that keeps detail close up. Stochastic stays one choice away: the pilot's Render setting, a link's
   `?stochastic`, the scene's own entry, or the owner's preset of a class (a desktop with a strong GPU is the only place the engine runs it).
2. **No per-scene hack, and no "stochastic only for big scans" rule**: the cost side does not support one on this hardware. Sorting 4 M splats
   costs 0.85 to 2.1 ms a frame at 1280x720 and 3.9 to 5.6 ms at 4K (v0.9 `v09-r-cost-3840x2160.json`), against 16.7 ms of a 60 Hz frame;
   stochastic saves 25 to 45 % of that, about 1.5 to 2.4 ms. Not a bottleneck on a strong GPU. Not measured: a weak GPU, where sorting is
   proportionally more expensive. That is why the built-in presets for the weaker classes start lower (budget, resolution) instead of
   switching drawing: the governor lowers both when frames are missed and cannot make a grainy picture good.

## Design

### Device classes (B), `packages/render-pc/src/device-class.ts`

`classifyDevice(signals)` -> `{ cls, reasons }`, pure, tested; `apps/fly/src/app/drawing.ts` reads the browser (`deviceSignals`), the WebGPU
adapter comes from the engine's own device (`SplatRenderer.gpuInfo`: vendor, architecture, device, description, fallback flag,
`maxBufferSize`). Four classes:

| class | rule |
|---|---|
| `phone` | a handheld (`isHandheld`: Client Hints `mobile`, Android / iPhone / iPad / iPod / "Mobile" in the user agent, iPadOS Safari = "Macintosh" with touch points) that is phone-sized: iPhone or iPod, Android with "Mobile", or any handheld whose short screen side is under 600 CSS px. Also a phone-sized touch screen behind a DESKTOP user agent ("Request desktop site" in Android Chrome, some Samsung modes): more than one touch point and `screen` short side under 600 CSS px (`isSmallTouchScreen`; reason `small-touch-screen`). A touch laptop (768 and up) is not caught; a tablet in desktop mode looks like a Linux touch laptop and is not looked for (the pilot names the class) |
| `tablet` | any other handheld |
| `desktop-weak` | a desktop browser with no WebGPU (the engine's CPU-sort renderer), a fallback (software) adapter, an Intel integrated GPU (Arc and Xe-HPG are discrete: strong), or two of: `deviceMemory` at most 4 GB, at most 4 logical cores, adapter `maxBufferSize` under 1 GiB |
| `desktop-strong` | every other desktop |

What it cannot see: an AMD APU or a Qualcomm laptop whose browser hides the adapter's name falls on the hints alone (this PC's Chrome reports
vendor and architecture and an empty description; Chrome caps `deviceMemory` at 8). A wrong guess is cheap: the quality governor lowers
budget and resolution from the preset when frames are missed, and the pilot can name the class himself (Settings > Display > Device class).
On this PC: vendor `nvidia`, architecture `lovelace`, 36 cores, 32 GB (reported as 8) -> `desktop-strong`.

### Presets (B), `packages/render-pc/src/presets.ts`

A preset = `{ drawing: 'sorted' | 'stochastic', budgetM: 0.25..32, renderScale: 0.4..1 }`, the top values the quality governor lowers from.
Built-in table (`BUILTIN_PRESETS`):

| class | drawing | budget | resolution cap | where the number comes from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| desktop-strong | sorted | 4 M | 100 % | SuperSplat's viewer's desktop budget and the v0.9 value (section 5: 8 M costs +65 to 71 % bytes for +6 dB on big scans, the owner's call) |
| desktop-weak | sorted | 2.5 M | 85 % | the governor's own ladder (1, 0.85, 0.7, 0.6, 0.5 / 4, 3, 2.5, 2, 1.5 M) entered where a weak device settles, so it does not spend its first seconds missing frames on the way down. NOT MEASURED on a weak GPU |
| tablet | sorted | 2 M | 85 % | SuperSplat's viewer's mobile budget (v0.8). NOT MEASURED on a tablet |
| phone | sorted | 2 M | 70 % | the same, and a phone's pixel ratio of 3 makes full resolution the heaviest part. NOT MEASURED on a phone |

The owner overrides any FIELD of any class (the rest stays the built-in value). Stored with the catalogue.

### The order of who decides (4), unchanged except the last step

link (`?budget=`, `?stochastic`) > the pilot's settings (Render, Splat budget) > the scene's own entry (`render` in the catalogue) > **the class's
preset** (was: "stochastic where the device can"). `resolveDrawing` (render-pc `drawing.ts`) takes the device with its class and preset; a
device that cannot draw stochastic (a phone, a tablet, no WebGPU) draws sorted whatever the preset says, with the v0.9 note when somebody ASKED
for it (a preset's default is not news). The refused-device budget is the class's preset budget (was SuperSplat's viewer's rule; an iPad is
now the tablet class, 2 M, where it was 4 M) **unless the pilot set a Splat budget: his number holds on every device, up as well as down**
(review round: the first cut kept it only when lighter than the preset, so an iPad Pro or a strong GPU behind a browser without WebGPU could
not be raised by the control that is shown; the quality governor still lowers any top when frames are missed). A link's or a scene's budget is
still refused there (a desktop number written for somebody else's machine) and the HUD note still says so.

Why a desktop without WebGPU starts as `desktop-weak` (2.5 M at 85 %, where v0.9 drew 4 M at 100 %): the site tells that browser flying needs
WebGPU (`banner.noWebgpu`), so it looks at the scan on the engine's CPU-sort renderer, whose cost no GPU hint can see, and nobody measured a
strong GPU behind such a browser. It starts cautious; the pilot's Splat budget lifts the budget (above), the Device class setting lifts the
resolution cap too. Not measured on a WebGL machine; owner question 2 covers the number.

### The admin's "Drawing" tab (B, 4), `apps/fly/src/admin/drawing-tab.ts`, `apps/api/admin.py`

- Storage: the catalogue document gets a third key, `drawing: { presets: { <class>: { drawing?, budgetM?, renderScale? } } }`, so the
  draft, Publish, the history and Restore are the catalogue's own (nothing new to deploy but `admin.py`). `clean_drawing` keeps known
  classes, known modes and numbers in range and drops the rest; a client that sends no `drawing` key (an older admin page) does not wipe it,
  one that sends it empty removes it (back to the built-in table). `GET /api/catalog` carries `drawing` when set; absent = the built-in table.
- The tab: one row per class (drawing, budget, resolution, the built-in value under it, a "changed" mark, Reset), "Reset every preset", the order
  of who decides, and a table of every scene's own entry (the same `render` the scene's editor writes) with a filter; the sticky bar, the
  changes list and Publish count the drawing defaults too (`catalogDiff` has a `drawing` flag).
- The simulator reads `catalog.drawing` at boot (`setDrawingDefaults`), before any scene opens.

### The page (C), Settings > Display

Render and Splat budget as before (Auto = the class's preset); new `display.deviceClass` (Auto, desktop-strong, desktop-weak, tablet, phone);
under the Render row a live line: "This device: desktop, strong graphics (nvidia lovelace). Auto draws sorted, up to 4 M splats, at 100 % of
