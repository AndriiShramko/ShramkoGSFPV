## Findings

### I1 (keyboard, fixed) The survey's digits answered the lesson's pending question as well

- Defect. The mode installs a `keydown` handler on the window (capture phase) when it starts, so that a digit answers the question a lesson has asked (module 0 asks all the time) and 0 skips it. The recorder's survey puts its own capture on the same window much later, when a recording is stopped, and says in its header that it takes every key "before the key router and keyboard flying". A browser runs the listeners of one target and phase in the order they were added, and `stopImmediatePropagation` only keeps the ones AFTER it from running: the mode's handler, added first, ran first. So a recording stopped while the lesson was asking (the owner's school test: children spend the first session in module 0) gave the survey's "1" for yes to the lesson as an answer, and every digit after it to the next question, with the lesson's feedback and its next question spoken over the survey. The same held for the start screen over a flight, a replay and the pause menu: any digit pressed while the flight was held answered the question behind them.
- Probe (refuting attempt, real page: release build `d63663e` behind `serve-csp.mjs`, headless Chrome 156 with a fake microphone, keyboard only, `tools/bench/src/sonic-int-review.ts r1`, evidence `evidence/2026-11-08/sonic-int-review-r1-before.json`): ears-headphones, T T records, the intro skipped with N, the first question pending, T stops, the survey is offered, 1, 3, 3 pressed. The lesson received `answer ears-headphones:1:1:side value 1 via key` and `asking` went false after the "yes": 3 of 4 checks, the failing one this. Control in a page of its own (no survey): the same digit answers the question, so the probe can see a lesson answer.
- Test that failed before the fix: `tools/bench/test/sonic-int-digits.test.ts` (5 tests), the mode's handler and the survey's capture on one fake window that runs listeners in the order they were added, as a browser does. Before: "while the survey runs its digits answer the survey and not the lesson" failed (`expected [ 1 ] to deeply equal []`). Controls that pass before and after: with the flight running a digit answers (and 0 skips); a handler that does not look at the pause (the feature as it was) runs before the survey's capture and answers for it; a held key, a shortcut, a text field and a page with no question are not answers.
- Fix: the handler moved out of `sonic/feature.ts` into `sonic/digit-key.ts` (`digitAnswerHandler`, same rules) and told whether the flight is held (`ctx.session.paused`): a question waits with the flight, and a digit belongs to what is on top of it (the survey, the start screen, a replay, the pause menu). The survey holds the flight (`ctx.pause('panel')`) before it listens, so nothing else is needed on its side. After the survey the flight goes on and the question is still waiting.
- Checks run: `sonic-int-digits` 5, `sonic-mode`, `sonic-lessons` (37 tests in the three files); typecheck of every package; architecture; page run on a release build of the tree with the fix: 4 of 4 (`evidence/2026-11-08/sonic-int-review-r1.json`: no lesson answer, `asking` still true after the survey's digits).

### I2 (privacy, fixed) The consent question was queued behind the lesson's speech, and the reading limit let a second press agree to it unheard

- Defect. The recorder's question (`rec.minor`, `rec.ask`) and U's (`send.confirm`) were said with no interrupt. The engine speaks with the browser's own voice, the default of a pilot: utterances queue one after another, and only a line that interrupts cuts what is queued. A lesson says four to six lines when it opens (10 to 24 s at the normal speed, measured by the package's own estimate for the 21 lessons; a pilot who slows the voice down makes it longer), so a T pressed in that time got no answer until the lesson had finished talking. And the recorder waits for the end of its question before a press counts (the F1 rule of the feedback review: "a key struck twice in a hurry agrees to nothing"), with a reading limit of 20 s so that a hidden tab cannot lock the pilot out: that limit runs on a timer from the call, not from the moment the question is spoken. A second press after 20 s, with the question still queued, was therefore taken as the yes: `getUserMedia` was called and the recording started before the pilot had heard the question. U's question has the same limit and the same hole (its yes sends the package, with the voice, to the author).
- Probe (refuting attempt, real page: release build of the tree, headless Chrome 156, the engine speaking with the browser's own voice at speed 0.75, `walls-hall` open and saying its intro, `speechSynthesis.speak` watched; `tools/bench/src/sonic-int-review.ts r2`, evidence `evidence/2026-11-08/sonic-int-review-r2-before.json`): T pressed 1.5 s into the intro; the question started to be spoken at +26.3 s (after the intro); a second T at +21.0 s, just after the reading limit, started the recording at +21.3 s, 5 s before the question was spoken. 2 of 4 checks (the question within 2 s of T; no yes before the question).
- Tests that failed before the fix: `sonicrec-recorder.test.ts` "review I2" (3 tests, a fake engine that queues utterances as the browser's does and cuts the queue for a line that interrupts): the first line of the question carries `interrupt` (a minor's second line follows it), and with six lesson lines queued (240 ms against a reading limit of 90 ms) the question begins within 30 ms of the press and a press after the limit is a yes to a question that had begun; `sonicrec-send.test.ts` "review I2" (3 tests): U's question, "nothing to send", "delivered" and where the sending is are said with interrupt, the lines the sending says by itself as it goes are not, and the same queued-lesson scenario for U. Controls that pass before and after: the same scenario said the old way (no interrupt) shows the question starting after more than 200 ms and the second press agreeing to it (recorder: the microphone is asked for; U: the package is stored).
- Fix: `RecorderDeps.say` takes `{ interrupt }` (`session.ts` passes it through to the engine); `Recorder.ask()` says the first line of the question with interrupt (the second line of a minor's question follows it); `SendControl.sayLine(key, params, answer)` says the answers to the pilot's own U with interrupt (the question, `send.none`, `send.delivered` for an already delivered recording, where a running sending is, `rec.minor` when the guardian is missing) and leaves the lines the sending says by itself to queue. A lesson whose intro was cut by T is opened again from its beginning when the recording starts (step 5), so a yes loses nothing; a no (or no answer) leaves the lesson without the rest of its intro, as an I, a Z or an N already do.
- Checks run: `sonicrec-*` (10 files, 244 tests), typecheck of every package; page run on a release build of the tree with the fix: 4 of 4 (`evidence/2026-11-08/sonic-int-review-r2.json`: the question starts 54 ms after T).

### I3 (a seam of step 5, fixed) A recording that started in the glossary lost the way back to the lesson G had left

- Defect. A helper who starts the recording while the child is in the glossary sends the child on to the next lesson of the path, away from the one G left.
- Test that failed before the fix: `sonic-lessons.test.ts` "review I3" (the lesson rig: rings-first, G, the glossary opened again as a recording starts, N through it): the page was on `ears-walls`, `expected 'ears-walls' to be 'rings-first'`. ### I4 (privacy, accessibility, fixed) A minor's two-line question was "read out" after the longer line, with the screen reader still on the question

- Defect. With "my screen reader speaks" (the engine's `selfVoice` off) the engine says nothing itself: `say()` waits the estimate of its line (`estimateSpeechS`) from the call, every line on its own, all at once (package `audio/engine.ts`), while a screen reader reads the captions one after the other. Step 6 of v12-int made the lesson host wait for its lines one after the other in that mode, but the recorder's own question did not: a minor's question is two lines (`rec.minor`, the guardian's consent; `rec.ask`, the question), handed over together, so the pair counted as read out after the longer of the two (7.8 s at speed 1) while a reader needs both (14.9 s). A hurried second press in those 7 s was the yes, with the recorder asking for the microphone before the pilot (a child) had heard the question itself. The same rule as I2, through the other door.
- Probe (refuting attempt, real page: release build before the fix, headless Chrome 156, engine silent at speed 1, a profile under 18 with the guardian confirmed, `walls-hall`; `tools/bench/src/sonic-int-review.ts r3`, evidence `evidence/2026-11-08/sonic-int-review-r3-before.json`): a second T at +10.7 s (after the longer line, before the pair was read at 14.9 s) started the recording. 3 of 4 checks, the failing one this. Control: a press after both lines is the yes.
- Tests that failed before the fix: `sonicrec-recorder.test.ts` "review I4" (3 tests, a fake of the engine in that mode: each line waits its own time from the call, in parallel): a press between the end of the longer line and the end of the pair is not a yes (`expected 1 to be +0`), and the second line is handed over when the first is over (it was handed over at once). Control that passes before and after: an adult's one line and the words and order of a minor's two. The older test "for a minor the guardian line and the question are both read out before a press counts" now expects the question to be handed over when the guardian line is over (its gates did not change otherwise).
- Fix: `Recorder.ask()` hands the question over when the guardian line has settled (with interrupt: nothing of ours is left to cut), and counts the pair as read when both have; a `say` that returns nothing (the fakes) has nothing to wait for and gets both lines at once as before. With a voice that queues (speechSynthesis) the two lines followed each other anyway.
- Checks run: `sonicrec-*` (10 files, 247 tests), typecheck; page run on a release build with the fix: 4 of 4 (`evidence/2026-11-08/sonic-int-review-r3.json`: the early press is no yes, the pair read, a press is the yes at +16.5 s).
- Not changed: the estimate is not the reader's time (NVDA, JAWS and VoiceOver read at their own speed; none was run), so a pilot whose reader is slower than the app's estimate can still press early; the reading limit (20 s) is as it was.

## Left open (found, not fixed: a design choice, too small, or not provable here)

1. The session chunk (the recorder, about 56 KB gzip) can fail to load with nothing said: `app/builtin/sonic-session.ts` retries every 5 s for ever and neither the pilot (T does nothing) nor the helper (no panel) hears or sees a word. The start and mode chunks say `sonic.say.lessonFailed`; the session's one would need a new key in the main dictionaries, and the main chunk has 323 bytes of gzip left of the +1.5 % target (850,945 of 851,267).
2. `rec.started`, `rec.mic-denied`, `rec.stopped` and `rec.saved` are said without interrupt, behind whatever the lesson is saying: the pilot who has just said yes can wait seconds to hear that the microphone is live.
