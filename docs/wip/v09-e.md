# v0.9 lane E — input delay over Bluetooth (the owner's item 12)

Branch `v09-e`, base `631fd5f`. Plan: [`v09-plan.md`](v09-plan.md). Owns: `packages/input` sampling and
timestamps, `apps/fly/src/devices/**` (not keyboard.ts), `apps/fly/src/latency.ts`, the radio check
screen's report-rate line.

## The question

The owner flies a Radiomaster GX12 over Bluetooth (the ExpressLRS module's "BLE Joystick" mode, which the
PC sees as a plain gamepad: "8 axis 0 button gamepad") and reports about 300 ms between moving a stick and
the green stick on the screen moving; the drone is late too. He set no expo and no rates. v0.8 changed how
we sample a gamepad (stamping with `Gamepad.timestamp`). Questions: (1) does OUR code add delay on a
Bluetooth-like timeline; (2) if yes, fix it; (3) show the pilot the measured report interval so the rest
is visible and honest; (4) say what Bluetooth itself costs and what he can do.

## The path of one stick move (what we control, what we do not)

```
stick -> radio -> [ELRS module: one BLE notification per CRSF frame] -> BLE link (connection interval) ->
Windows BLE HID driver -> Chrome raw-input window (WM_INPUT, stamps Gamepad.timestamp) ->
Chrome gamepad provider publishes every 4 ms -> getGamepads() in the page        <- we start here
-> GamepadSource.poll (MessageChannel loop, >= 2 ms apart) -> Controls.raw -> session.input -> SimClock queue
-> the next animation frame steps the physics (applied at its stamp) and draws -> compositor -> display
```

Left of the arrow: the device, the link and Chrome. Right of it: ours. The harness measures from the moment
the browser HAS the report; the link's own delay is before that and cannot be seen from the page.

## Answer in one paragraph (details and sources below)

Our code does not add the 300 ms. On every link timeline we ran (USB 1000 Hz, Bluetooth LE ~90 Hz with
jitter, Bluetooth in bunches, a controller that reports only changes) our poll finds a move 1.0 ms after
the browser published it (2.1 ms at worst), and v0.8's stamping made the physics hear of it 2-3 ms sooner
than v0.7 (6.6 ms sooner under a heavy scan). The one delay we did add was a `Gamepad.timestamp` that does
not move while the values do: 10 ms on average, 21 ms at worst, in v0.7 and v0.8 alike; fixed (1.0 ms). The
rest of what he sees is the 30 Hz screen (the stick is drawn 18-20 ms after the report arrives at 30 Hz,
11 ms at 60 Hz, and then the display adds its own frames) and the Bluetooth link (a report only at a
connection event, 7.5-50 ms apart; an Xbox pad measures 5-6 ms slower over Bluetooth than by cable and an
ESP32 BLE gamepad about 19 ms in third-party tests, a link in trouble much more). The Controls screen and F3 now show how often his reports really
arrive, so the link can be told from the screen without a camera.

## Where our code could add delay: hypotheses and what the harness found

`tools/bench/test/pad-timeline.test.ts` (model: `helpers/pad-sim.ts`) runs the REAL `GamepadSource` on a
virtual clock and a virtual main thread against a pad modelled on Chromium's gamepad path (a report is
stamped at its arrival; the provider publishes the newest state every 4 ms; `getGamepads()` reads the last
published state). Three versions: v0.7 (`097300d`) and v0.8 (`631fd5f`) as fixtures copied from git, and the
file in `apps/fly`. 100 stick moves per scenario, off any frame rhythm; the 30 Hz screen is the owner's.
`tools/bench/src/pad-timeline.ts` prints the whole table and writes `evidence/2026-10-17/v09-e-pad-timeline.json`.

- **H1 confirmed and fixed.** `fresh` was `Gamepad.timestamp !== lastTs` only; a browser or device whose
  timestamp does not move while the values do was noticed at the 20 ms keep-alive (v0.7 and v0.8 alike, so
  not a v0.8 regression). A value that moved is now a new sample whatever the timestamp says.
- **H2 v0.8's stamping never adds delay, and saves 2-3 ms** (6.6 ms under a heavy scan): it applies a move at
  the report's arrival instead of the poll. One hole: a timestamp that goes BACKWARDS made v0.8 stamp a move up
  to 30 ms before it happened; a timestamp before the previous sample's own is now not trusted (poll time).
  Stale (> 100 ms) and future timestamps already fell back to the poll time. The rest of v0.8's changes on
  the input path (`git diff 097300d 631fd5f` over `controls.ts`, `calib.ts`, `mode-switch.ts`, `switches.ts`,
  the known-controller table, the stick-end snap at 85 %) was read line by line: names, tables and value
  scaling, no smoothing, filtering, waiting or queueing. The physics applies a queued sample at the first tick
  at or after its stamp (`Runner.stepOnce`), a past stamp at the very next tick, so a stamp never rewinds or
  holds anything back.
- **H3 bursty links**: the browser's provider publishes the newest state every 4 ms, so a bunch of reports
  within 1.5 ms is ONE new sample for the page; nothing is held back (the bunches' own 50-100 ms cadence is
  the link's). The stick is drawn 18 ms after it.
- **H4 polling is already off the animation frame** (a MessageChannel loop, since v0.2). The control:
  reading the pad only once per frame puts the pad 17 ms behind (33 ms at worst) and hands the physics 30
  points a second of the stick's path instead of ~100-500; the DRAWN stick would not be later (a frame reads
  the freshest value), which is why the loop matters for input resolution, the arm gate and the wizard, not
  for the picture.
- **H5 the screen**: at 30 Hz the stick is drawn 18-20 ms after the report arrives (2 ms provider tick +
  1 ms poll + the wait for the frame), at 60 Hz 11 ms; the picture reaches the display after that (not in the
  table; the same for every version). Cannot be lowered in a browser.
- **What our poll still costs** is 1.0 ms mean because the loop looks every 2 ms. It makes 2 % of the moves
  (4 % under a heavy scan, 7 % at 60 Hz) miss the frame they could have been in: a whole display period
  (33 ms at 30 Hz). Reading the pad once more just before the frame draws and steps the physics (`pollNow`)
  takes that to 0 %.

Controls (each must fail where it should): v0.7 shows the poll delay in its stamp (2.8-3.1 ms against 0-1.3);
v0.7 and v0.8 show the 10 ms of the frozen timestamp; v0.8 shows the early stamp of a backwards timestamp; a
pad read only once per frame comes out 17 ms behind; a polling loop that takes 8 ms per pass shows up as +3 ms;
the 60 Hz screen draws 8 ms sooner than the 30 Hz one.

## What changed

- `apps/fly/src/devices/gamepad.ts`: a moved axis or button under a frozen timestamp is a sample; a timestamp
  before the previous one is not trusted (`gamepadSampleTime`); `pollNow()`; every new sample is noted in
  `link` (a `LinkMeter`); the loop's own stalls are noted too.
- `packages/input/src/link.ts` (new, no DOM): `LinkMeter` measures the gaps between a device's new samples:
  mean (rate), jitter (standard deviation), longest gap, `ageMs` (how old the newest value is on average:
  half a gap for a steady device, more when it bunches), the share of bunched gaps, and one verdict
  (`ok` / `slow` above 10 ms / `bursty`). It leaves out silences over 250 ms (a device that reports only
  changes is idle, not slow) and the page's own stalls (WebHID events queue behind a busy main thread and
  would make a 1000 Hz cable look bunched: `devices/link-beat.ts` is the witness timer).
- `apps/fly/src/devices/hid.ts`: the same meter on WebHID reports.
- Flight: `FlightSession.beforeStep` (called at the start of each frame) runs the gamepad's `pollNow`
  (`Controls.useDevice`, set by `app/input.ts`). Controls screen: its draw loop calls `pollNow` too.
- The Controls screen's footer (`ui/radio.ts`, `ui/link-text.ts`) and F3 (`ui/hud.ts`) show "reports every
  X ms · jitter Y ms · longest gap Z ms" (F3 also the rate); for a verdict `slow` or `bursty` also the note
  "Bluetooth adds delay — a USB cable is faster" with the measured numbers (`locales/fly/link/`, en/es/pl/ru).
  Verified in Chrome on a release build: `tools/bench/scripts/link-line-probe.mjs` (a fake pad on four
  timelines; a cable gets no note on either screen).

## Limits of the meter and of the model (stated, not hidden)

- The page sees the cadence of NEW values as the browser publishes them. A bunch of reports within 4 ms is
  one sample on the Gamepad API (WebHID shows each report). The poll limits a readout to 2 ms: a cable at
  1000 Hz reads "every 2 ms".
- A device that reports only changes (the DJI) is measured while the stick moves; its idle seconds are left out.
- A browser path that stamps every poll (Chromium's Windows.Gaming.Input fetcher sets `Gamepad.timestamp` at
  read time; it serves Xbox-style gamepads, not an ELRS joystick) makes any pad read as 250 Hz: the page
  cannot see the device behind it (pinned as a known limit in the harness tests).
- The model of Chromium is from reading its source (below), not from a trace on the owner's PC. The harness
  starts at the browser having the report: the link's delay, the link's queue and the display's own frames are
  outside it. WebHID's `event.timeStamp` is when the page ran the event, so a busy page stamps its moves late
  (v0.8 note D42: 15.5 / 29.7 ms under load); nothing in the page can know the true arrival there.

## Research: what is normal, with sources

**Chromium (read 2026-10-17).** The gamepad provider thread polls every 4 ms
(`kPollingIntervalMilliseconds = 4`, [gamepad_provider.cc](https://github.com/chromium/chromium/blob/main/device/gamepad/gamepad_provider.cc)).
A generic HID joystick (an ELRS BLE joystick is one) goes through the raw-input fetcher: a message window gets
`WM_INPUT` per report and `GetGamepadData` reads the latest state, no polling of the device
([raw_input_data_fetcher_win.cc](https://github.com/chromium/chromium/blob/main/device/gamepad/raw_input_data_fetcher_win.cc));
`pad.timestamp` is the time the report was processed
([raw_input_gamepad_device_win.cc](https://github.com/chromium/chromium/blob/main/device/gamepad/raw_input_gamepad_device_win.cc)),
so Gamepad.timestamp is the report's arrival. The Windows.Gaming.Input fetcher (Xbox-style pads) stamps at
read time, `CurrentTimeInMicroseconds()` on every poll
([wgi_data_fetcher_win.cc](https://github.com/chromium/chromium/blob/main/device/gamepad/wgi_data_fetcher_win.cc)).
Blink takes a fresh sample on every `getGamepads()` call, nothing is cached per frame
([navigator_gamepad.cc](https://github.com/chromium/chromium/blob/main/third_party/blink/renderer/modules/gamepad/navigator_gamepad.cc)),
and converts the timestamp to a DOMHighResTimeStamp on the page's time origin, clamped to a floor
([gamepad.cc](https://github.com/chromium/chromium/blob/main/third_party/blink/renderer/modules/gamepad/gamepad.cc)):
the same clock as `performance.now()`, which is what `gamepadSampleTime` assumes.

**ExpressLRS BLE joystick.** The module registers the sender as the handset's RC-data callback and calls
`bleGamepad->sendReport()` on EVERY callback, with no rate limit: 8 axes and 8 buttons from the channels
([devBLE.cpp](https://github.com/ExpressLRS/ExpressLRS/blob/master/src/lib/BLE/devBLE.cpp)). So the report rate
the radio hands the module (its CRSF frame rate) is not the rate the BLE link can carry. Windows lists it as
"8 axis 0 button gamepad", range about 3 m, "highly dependent on the Bluetooth card and drivers"
([Oscar Liang's guide](https://oscarliang.com/expresslrs-bluetooth-joystick/); no latency figure there);
forum users call the latency "not great" and prefer a cable
([IntoFPV, ELRS 3.5.1 thread](https://intofpv.com/t-expresslrs-3-5-1-now-available)). Anecdote, not measurement.

**Bluetooth LE HID.** A notification leaves only at a connection event, so a report waits up to one
connection interval; for HID over GATT the interval must be 7.5-50 ms
([TI SWRA715](https://www.ti.com/document-viewer/lit/html/SWRA715/GUID-3E3206EB-BACF-4F54-AEFE-0C98C5B25389),
[Nordic DevZone](https://devzone.nordicsemi.com/f/nordic-q-a/84065/nrf52832-ble-hids-keyboard--measure-the-bluetooth-report-rate/349840)).
The central (the PC) sets the parameters: one Nordic thread saw a Windows 10 central stay at 20 ms whatever the
peripheral asked ([Nordic DevZone](https://devzone.nordicsemi.com/f/nordic-q-a/27235/win-10-ble-stacks-acting-as-a-central-problems-accepting-ble-connection-parameter-changes);
an old forum anecdote). I found no Microsoft document that states the interval Windows uses, and no measurement of
Windows BLE gamepad latency: **what Windows does is not established here.** Apple documents 11.25 ms for BLE HID
accessories (not Windows).

**Measured Bluetooth gamepads (third party).** gamepadla.com (a hardware rig, a tool still in testing) lists
an Xbox Series pad at about 6.5 ms by cable and 12.2 ms over Bluetooth for a button, 8.3 and 13 ms for a stick
([comparison](https://gamepadla.com/versus/8bitdo-ultimate-bluetooth-controller_vs_xbox-core-controller.html)); the
ESP32-BLE-Gamepad library the ELRS joystick is built on lists about 18.6 ms median button latency in its own
hardware-in-the-loop test ([README](https://github.com/lemmingDev/ESP32-BLE-Gamepad)). So a healthy Bluetooth
link costs roughly 5-20 ms in those tests, not hundreds. (Gamepads, not the ELRS joystick: not measured.)

**USB.** EdgeTX runs its mixer at 1000 Hz in USB Joystick mode
([EdgeTX v2.10 changes](https://manual.edgetx.org/v2.10/summary_of_changes),
[USB Joystick](https://manual.edgetx.org/color-radios/model-settings/model-setup/usb-joystick)); a full-speed USB
HID endpoint polls every 1 ms at best. I found no published report rate of the radio's USB endpoint.

**The owner's screen (our own measurements).** A 4K monitor at 30 Hz over HDMI: v0.3's A9 run measured input to
screen at 252 ms median, 295 ms p95 on this display ([evidence/2026-09-24/a9-latency.json](../../evidence/2026-09-24/a9-latency.json));
v0.5 cut it to about 83 ms without GPU load and 120-130 ms under a 40 ms GPU load, the floor of a 30 Hz screen
([evidence/2026-10-02/v05-latency.json](../../evidence/2026-10-02/v05-latency.json)); `hud.slowDisplay` says
60 Hz about halves it. None of these include the link.

## Why 300 ms: a budget, with what is known and what is not

| stage | ms | known how |
|---|---|---|
| the radio, the ELRS module, the CRSF frame | not known | no hardware here |
| Bluetooth link | 5-20 in third-party gamepad tests; the ELRS joystick not measured; more when it queues | the tests above; the new line shows only the cadence, not a queue |
| browser: report arrives, provider tick | 2 mean (0-4) | Chromium source, modelled |
| our poll | 1 mean (0-2) | harness |
| frame wait, 30 Hz | 17 mean (0-33) | harness |
| display path on his screen (GPU, present, 30 Hz scan-out), in flight | 85-130 p50 in total with the frame wait | v0.5 evidence |

On the Controls screen (a DOM stick, no physics) the stick can show about 65 ms after the report reaches the
browser at best on his display (the blank-page floor A9 measured there: 65 ms median), more while the scan
behind it keeps the GPU busy; in flight about 85-130 ms. A cable would show the same. A healthy Bluetooth link
on top brings it to about 90-150 ms in all. About 300 ms needs 150 ms more than that: a link that queues
(hypothesis: the module sends a notification per CRSF frame without a limit, faster than a BLE link drains,
so a queue fills and every report waits behind it; **not verified, no hardware**), or a heavier scan than v0.5
measured on his 4K screen.
The new lines separate the two: a steady 8-12 ms cadence says the link delivers on time (then it is the screen
and the GPU); 30 ms or more, large jitter, long gaps or bunches say the link is the weak part.

## The honest answer for the owner

- **What we found and fixed on our side:** a gamepad whose timestamp does not move with its values was read
  10 ms late (21 ms at worst); a timestamp that goes backwards could stamp a move early; a move that arrived
  2 ms before a frame missed the frame in 2 % of the cases. All three are fixed and measured. v0.8's change
  made it better (2-3 ms), not worse. Our code does not add 300 ms.
- **What Bluetooth itself costs:** a healthy Bluetooth gamepad link is about 5-20 ms slower than a cable in
  third-party tests; the ELRS BLE joystick mode is reported by pilots as laggier than that, we could not
  measure it. It is the part
  we cannot see from the page except as a cadence: look at the line on the Controls screen.
- **What he can do, in order:** (1) open Controls with the radio on and read the new line: "every 8 ms or
  less" is not the link, "10 ms or more", gaps or bunches is. (2) A USB cable and EdgeTX's USB Joystick mode
  (the GX12 asks on plugging in; see the manual) is the fastest and has none of this. (3) The screen: a 4K
  monitor at 30 Hz is the larger part of his delay in flight (about 85-130 ms of it); DisplayPort or a lower
  resolution at 60 Hz about halves that. (4) For Bluetooth: radio within 1-2 m of the PC, no USB 3 hub or
  Wi-Fi router between, another Bluetooth adapter (the guide says the result depends on the adapter and
  drivers). (5) Worth trying, unverified: a lower ExpressLRS packet rate in BLE joystick mode, so the module
  sends fewer notifications into the link.

## An idea for the lead, not done here

While the Controls screen is open the scan keeps drawing behind it, and the DOM stick shares the GPU and the
compositor with that draw: on a 4K screen at 30 Hz the "green stick" may be later than it needs to be only
because of the scan. Drawing the scan at a low rate (or not at all) while Controls is up would show it; that
is the renderer's (lane A), and it was not measured.
