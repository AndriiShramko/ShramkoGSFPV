## Findings

### F1 (privacy, fixed) A second press of T agreed to recording before the question had been read out

`Recorder.pressRecord()` took the second press as the pilot's yes at once: `confirm()` called `getUserMedia` in that press. The first press only speaks `rec.ask` ("May I record this flight and your voice? ...", and `rec.minor` before it for a minor) and does not wait for it. Step 8 had the right rule for U ("a key struck twice in a hurry agrees to nothing": the question must have been read out) and the lane's own words for its U bug say a hurried press must not consent; T, which opens the microphone, did not have it. With the microphone permission already granted for the site (Chrome keeps it), a double tap of T started a voice recording with no prompt and no explanation heard, and for a minor skipped the guardian line.

Probe: four tests written first against the unchanged recorder (a say that settles only when the line ends, as the page's `sayVia` does): all four failed (the second press had asked for the microphone, `mic.asked` was 1 instead of 0; the time to answer ran out while the question was still being read). Fix, `apps/fly/src/sonic-session/recorder.ts` and `session.ts`: the recorder takes the promise `say` returns and counts a press as the answer only after every line of the question has been said or `readMs` (20 s, as for U) has passed, so a hidden tab cannot lock the pilot out; the time to answer (`askMs`, 30 s) starts when the question has been heard. A `say` that returns nothing (the Node fakes) means nothing to wait for, so every older test is unchanged. The helper's Start button is unchanged (its tick is the consent and nothing is read). Tests: `sonicrec-recorder.test.ts` 4 new (hurried press ignored, minor's two lines, the reading limit, the timer's start), `sonicrec-feature.test.ts` updated to wait for the question as a person must. Not judged: how long a pilot needs to hear the question.

### F2 (accessibility, fixed) The panel's live regions were rewritten every second while recording and four times a second while a replay was open

`panel.ts` put "Recording: 0:07. Flight frames: 210." into a `role="status" aria-live="polite"` paragraph and redrew it every second (the recorder's status timer). A screen reader reads out every change of a polite live region, so a helper (or a blind pilot who runs a screen reader beside the app's own voice) would hear a new clock reading every second for the whole flight, on top of the cues. Worse, `replay-view.ts` redraws every 250 ms while a replay is open (the position moves) and its `draw()` wrote `status.textContent` each time, and the same tick called the panel's `drawStatus()`, so two live sentences ("The replay agrees with ...", "Not recording.") were written four times a second with the same text; `textContent = same text` replaces the text node and is announced as much as a new text.

Probe, `tools/bench/src/sonicrec-review.ts` R1 (headless Chrome 156, the real panel on the lane's page; a MutationObserver counts writes inside live regions): before the fix, 3.5 s of steady recording gave 3 writes of 3 different texts in `sonicrec-status`; an open replay that was not even playing gave 9 writes of ONE text in `sonicrec-replay-status` and 9 of one text in `sonicrec-status` in 2.2 s. After: 0 and 0 (both checks pass). Fix: the status sentence carries no number (`sonicrec.status.recording` is "Recording." in en, es, pl, ru) and the clock and frame counter have a paragraph of their own that is not live (`sonicrec.clock`, new key, four languages); every write the two redraw loops make goes through `setText`, which writes only a different text; the replay's notes list and the slider's `aria-valuetext` are written only when they change. Held in Node by `tools/bench/test/sonicrec-review.test.ts` (no live sentence has `{time}`, `{frames}` or `{size}`; `setText` and its control). Regression: `sonicrec-local.ts` 32 of 32 after the fix (its evidence files were restored: not re-committed), `packages/i18n` tests, typecheck. Not done, on purpose: the toggle button's text still shows the clock ("Recording 0:07"); it is not a live region, so nothing reads it out unless it has the focus. Not judged: what a real screen reader says (none was used here).

### F3 (privacy, fixed) "Recording stopped." was said at the stop, but the microphone listened on through the whole survey

The spec (section 9) wants the survey's open answers spoken into the same recording, so after the pilot's T the voice keeps recording until the survey ends (`sonicrec-local.ts` E6a asserts exactly that). But `doStop()` said the package's `rec.stopped` ("Recording stopped.") at the stop. For up to a minute of silence on the offer ("a minute of silence is no") and for the whole survey after a yes, everything the pilot and the helper said (the debrief, a private remark) went into a voice track that is then sent to Telegram and to e-mail, after the app had said that recording stopped. Probe (a test written first, failed on the old recorder): while the survey runs the microphone is not let go (`mic.stopped` 0) and `rec.stopped` had already been said.

Fix, `recorder.ts`: `rec.stopped` is said in `finish()` right after the microphone has been let go (the same place where "saved" follows), so the pilot's first words after T are the survey's offer and the sentence is true when it is said; with no survey nothing changes (stop, saved). The privacy section of the site now says it, in en, es, pl, ru (one added sentence in the first paragraph: the microphone stays on while the survey is asked and goes off when it ends or is declined; the es, pl and ru are my translations, read by no native speaker). Tests: `sonicrec-recorder.test.ts` 2 new (the order while the survey runs and after; the no-survey control), `sonicrec-review.test.ts` (the privacy sentence in four languages), `sonicrec-review.ts` R2 in headless Chrome (while the offer is open: phase closing, voice recording, spoken `rec.ask, rec.started, survey.offer`; after declining: `survey.skipped, rec.stopped, rec.saved`).

Not fixed, for the owner to weigh (a design choice of the spec, not a defect of the code): the offer waits 60 s for a key while the microphone listens, and the survey offer line itself does not say the microphone is on. A spoken line for that would have to come from sonic-core (see "Needs from sonic-core" below).

### F4 (security, fixed) `start` accepted a body of any content type, so a page on another site could fill the upload quota through its visitors

`POST /api/session/start` is the only route a stranger can call with no token, and it reserves the whole declared size (up to 100 MiB, six per client address, 48 in all, out of 2 GiB) for 24 hours. `Handler._body()` reads the bytes whatever the `Content-Type`, so `fetch(url, { method: "POST", mode: "no-cors", body: JSON.stringify({ size: 104857600, sha256, consentSend: true }) })` from any web page is a "simple" cross-site request: no preflight, a `text/plain` body, sent from the visitor's own address, which is the key of the per-client limits. A page that enough people open could therefore reserve the 2 GiB and answer every real pilot with 507 `cap` for a day, without the sender owning one address. Probe (a test written first): a `text/plain` POST of a valid start body got 200 and a token (`self.store._open` held the reservation).

Fix, `server.py`: `start` is answered 415 `content-type` unless the body is `application/json` (a charset after it is fine). A JSON content type cannot be sent cross-site without a preflight, and nginx answers none (`limit_except GET POST` denies OPTIONS), so a browser cannot be made to call it. The page's own client already sends it (`upload.ts`), so nothing changes for pilots: the whole browser to API run, `sonicrec-upload.ts`, passes 35 of 35 after the change (its evidence files were restored: not re-committed). Test: `test_sessions.py` `Http` (4 content types refused, nothing reserved, nothing on disk; control: the same bytes as JSON start an upload); the `Http.call` helper had overridden every content type of a bytes body with octet-stream, so it now only sets a default, and `test_session_delivery.py`'s own helper sends the JSON type its client sends. API session, delivery, admin and nginx tests: 95 pass. Not fixed, by design of an anonymous upload: a direct attacker with several addresses can still hold the quota for a day; see "Left open" below.

### F5 (strings outside locales, fixed) The recorder's problems were English sentences built in the code, shown inside every language

`recorder.ts` kept its problems as English text (`'the sound mode is off'`, `` `only ${n} MB of browser storage are left, ${m} MB are needed` ``, `the voice could not be recorded: ...`, `the recording cannot be stored: ...`, `the survey stopped: ...`) and the panel put them into `sonicrec.error.generic` ("Problem: {reason}"). The rule is that a text for a person lives only in `packages/i18n/locales/**` in en, es, pl and ru; B18 cannot see English, so it passed. A Spanish, Polish or Russian helper who started a recording with too little browser storage read "Problema: only 4 MB of browser storage are left, 50 MB are needed" and nothing else.

Probe (browser, `sonicrec-review.ts` R3, the page in Spanish, `navigator.storage.estimate` saying 5 MB of 60 are left): before the fix the status line was the English sentence inside the Spanish frame (the four recorder tests that pinned the English text were changed first and failed on the old code); after, "Quedan solo 4 MB de almacenamiento del navegador y hacen falta 50 MB, ...". Fix: `RecorderStatus.error` is now `{ code, params }` (`RecorderError`: mode-off, storage-low, voice-start, voice-write, voice-stop, store, survey, finish, generic), the panel renders `sonicrec.error.<code>` with the params, and the eight new sentences are in all four dictionaries (the browser's own error text stays as the technical `{reason}`, which is what it is). `sonicrec-review.test.ts` holds that every code has a sentence in every language naming exactly the details the recorder gives it. The Spanish, Polish and Russian sentences are mine, read by no native speaker. Whole `sonicrec-*` and `packages/i18n` vitest run: 244 pass; typecheck of `apps/fly` and `tools/bench`.

Left open, small: the same English can still come through `sonicrec.msg.failed` ("Could not save: {reason}") when the registry throws (`another tab is recording this session`, `the recording cannot be packed: ...`; the message also says "save" for a failed delete or keep), and the native save dialog's file-type description in `save.ts` is the English "Fly by Sound recording". Both are one-line details; no test pins them.

### F6 (ordinary flight, fixed) The whole session side was in the main script of the simulator for every visitor

The lane's rule is "every hook is a no-op while the sound mode is off", and the hooks are. But `app/builtin/sonic-session.ts` imported `createSonicSession` statically, and the feature is installed for every visitor, so the recorder, the zip writer, the replay core, the panel, the upload client and the sound package's dictionaries in four languages (87 KB of Russian alone) were part of the one script every pilot downloads and parses before the first frame, mode or no mode. Measured with the release build (`pnpm --filter @gsfpv/fly build`, three builds of the same tree): main script 3 228 423 bytes (928 248 gzip, 734 781 brotli) with the session side, 2 857 839 (817 558 gzip, 645 410 brotli) with the feature stubbed out: the session side cost every visitor 370 584 bytes raw, 110 690 gzip, 89 371 brotli (+13 % of the script), for nothing. Nothing in the lane's checks looks at this: a6-reference and the flight tests do not care how big the script is. The lane's own summary of "ordinary flight is unchanged" was true of behaviour and not of weight.

Fix: the feature file imports the session side only through `import()` (the way `drone.ts` brings in the 3D drone models) and does it when `ctx.sonic` appears, at the next frame; until then it registers no key, no bus listener and no page listener. A load that fails (offline, a release replaced under the page) is tried again after 5 s; one that throws in `createSonicSession` leaves the feature off for the page and the flight alone; a feature taken out before the load ends installs nothing. Build after the fix: main script 2 858 235 bytes (817 744 gzip, 645 375 brotli: +396 bytes over the stubbed build), and a separate chunk `session-*.js` of 369 322 bytes (110 824 gzip, 95 541 brotli) plus `session-*.css` (5 679 bytes), which the page's HTML does not preload. Tests (`sonicrec-feature.test.ts`, written first, 4 failed on the old feature file): with the mode off after six frames T and U are nobody's (no handler, nothing loaded); when the mode starts the session side arrives and T asks and the second T (after the question) records; a feature taken out before the load installs nothing; the feature file has no static import of `sonic-session/` or the sound package (control: the old import line matches the rule). Also run: `a6-reference.test.ts` (the determinism hash) and the other app tests that load the features: pass; typecheck of `apps/fly`.

Not verified: the real flight page with the real mode (it needs the flight lane's `ctx.sonic`, a scan and a GPU; until the lead merges `v12-flight` nothing sets `ctx.sonic`, so in a real browser the session side stays unloaded, which is also what a visitor without the mode gets after the merge); a first press of T in the first moment after the mode starts (before the chunk has arrived, from the network, about 95 KB) is nobody's: the start screen and the question to the pilot take longer than that. For the lead: the flight lane's own feature is a bigger candidate for the same treatment (its engine, the lessons and worlds); `features.ts` imports it statically too.

### F7 (accessibility, fixed) The survey dialog was `role="dialog" aria-modal="true"` and never took the focus, and its first text was written while it was hidden

`survey-view.ts` built a modal dialog for the helper and drew it without any of what a modal needs. `aria-modal="true"` tells a screen reader to treat everything outside the dialog as not there, but the focus stayed on whatever it was on (the panel's button, the page), which is now outside: a keyboard or screen-reader helper could not reach the dialog's buttons at all (Tab leads away from them; the survey's own keys are captured at the window and swallow Tab too). And `offer()` and `question()` wrote the question into its `aria-live` paragraph and only then set `hidden = false`: a live region that changes while it is `display: none` is not announced and nothing is announced when it appears, so the offer, the first thing a helper using a screen reader needs, was silent (the pilot's own spoken line is the app's voice, not the helper's reader).

Probe (browser, `sonicrec-review.ts` R4, the real dialog; property setters logged by an init script): before the fix the focus was on `BUTTON.sonicrec-toggle` after the dialog opened, and the order was `question=empty, question=text, dialog.hidden=false`. After: focus on `SECTION.sonicrec-survey` and the order `question=empty, dialog.hidden=false, question=text`; when the survey is over the focus returns to the button it came from. Fix: the dialog has `tabindex="-1"`, `show()` remembers the focus and unhides before anything is written, `focusIn()` puts the focus on the dialog when it is not already inside it (a pressed button disappears when the next question is drawn), `close()` gives the focus back. The survey's keys and the flight pause are unchanged: `sonicrec-local.ts` 32 of 32 after the change (E6a to E6d are the survey), `sonicrec-review.ts` 8 of 8, typecheck. Not judged: what a screen reader says when the dialog takes the focus (none was used); the dialog now shows the default focus ring around itself when it opens by a key.
