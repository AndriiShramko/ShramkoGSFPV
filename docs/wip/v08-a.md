# v0.8 lane A: play — keyboard, touch, quieter sound, easy play

The owner's items 1, 2, 3 and 6 of message 21 ([v08-plan.md](v08-plan.md)). Branch `v08-a`. Radios,
gamepads and HID devices are lane B's and keep their arm switch and full control; nothing here changes
`packages/sim-core` (the A6 hash stays `1d01ca5b...`).

## 0. What the owner said, and what was found

1. **Keyboard is "impossible to control" for gamers.** Today's keys are a Mode 2 radio laid on the
   keyboard (W/S throttle that stays where it is left, A/D yaw, arrows pitch/roll, Space arm), every
   key bang-bang (0.6 stick the moment it goes down). A gamer must first find Space, then find the
   hover throttle by ear, then fly two half-sticks at once. Research (2026-10-16): there is no standard
   keyboard layout for drone games; the radio-on-keys layout is what pilot-oriented sims use
   (Dronesim, DJI Flight Simulator), and Liftoff's developer says keyboard flying is "practically
   impossible" for a realistic sim. Games for players use **WASD to move/tilt and the arrows for turn
   and altitude** (GoDrone's web UI: arrows up/down altitude, left/right yaw, W/S pitch, A/D roll; "The
   Dude": WASD tilt, arrows turn, Space/V up/down), often with Shift as a boost (NEXUS FPV) and an
   **altitude hold** (Dronesim's Shift). Sources: webxos.itch.io/nexus-fpv, bitona.itch.io/dronesim,
   godrone.readthedocs.io (first flight), steamcommunity.com/app/410340 (Liftoff, developer's answer).
2. **Touch sticks felt different from the radio.** Measured in code (section 3): the stick-to-setpoint
   path is the same (channels go into the same rates; no deadband or expo on either side; touch
   events are handled at their own timestamps), but the pads were **absolute**: a finger landing
   off-centre is an instant step on that stick, and every new touch on the left pad **set the throttle
   to where the thumb landed**. A radio never does either: the thumb lands on the stick where it is.
3. **Motor sound too loud by default.**
6. **Phones, tablets, keyboard: players, not pilots** — no arming; fun; feel like FPV; train for real sticks.

## 1. Play profile (`input.play`: `easy` by default, or `pilot`)

Chosen by the input type: **touch and keyboard** fly the profile in `input.play`; a radio, gamepad or
HID device always flies as a radio (arm switch, its own throttle). `pilot` is the old behaviour
(radio on keys / ARM button), for pilots who want it.

| | easy (default) | pilot |
|---|---|---|
| arming | automatic: the first flying key or touch starts the motors (throttle held low until the model says armed, so the sim's arm rule is met; a refused edge is retried) | Space / ARM button, throttle low, as a radio |
| keyboard | game layout: W/S forward/back, A/D left/right, arrows left/right (or Q/E) turn, arrows up/down climb/sink, Shift faster, Space motors off/on | Mode 2 layout: W/S throttle, A/D yaw, arrows pitch/roll (unchanged) |
| keys | smooth ramps (0 -> target in 0.25 s, back in 0.12 s), targets in physical units turned into stick through the drone's own rates: tilt 25 deg (Shift 45), acro 200 deg/s (Shift 400), turn 110 deg/s (Shift 220) | bang-bang 0.6 / 0.5 (unchanged) |
| throttle | altitude help on: hold height, climb/sink rate from the keys or the left stick (spring-centred); off: stays where it is left | stays where it is left |

**Altitude help** (`input.altitudeHelp`, on; only in easy play and in angle/horizon): a vertical-speed
controller in the page (`apps/fly/src/play/assist.ts`, pure): climb 2.5 m/s (Shift 5), sink 2 m/s
(Shift 4), and the height held when the command is released. Feed-forward from the model's own
numbers: thrust = mass (g + a) / cos(tilt), motor = sqrt(thrust / (4 tmax(V))), through idle and the
throttle curve (or the auto-throttle map) back to a stick; PI on the vertical speed. **Assists are
inputs to the sim**: the stick they compute goes through Controls and the arm gate into the flight log
like any other source, so replays stay exact without the assist.

**Pilot school** (`apps/fly/src/play/school.ts`): four steps read from the settings, so there is
nothing to get out of step: 1 angle + altitude help, 2 angle (own throttle), 3 horizon, 4 acro. Each
step has a goal in seconds of flight without a crash (30, 45, 60, 60); the HUD chip shows the step and
the progress; reaching it offers the next step (Next / Stay). After step 4: real sticks next (Controls).

**Haptics** (`input.haptics`, on): a short vibration on a crash with touch sticks (Android Chrome;
iOS Safari has no Vibration API).

## 2. Keyboard key card

The card shows while the craft waits on the ground (first time and every respawn that needs a
start) and on demand with **K** (keymap `help.keys`); it lists the layout of the profile in use.

## 3. Touch: the measurement and the fix

Relative grab in every profile: on touch-down the pad's origin is placed so the stick keeps its
value under the thumb (spring axes: origin = the touch point; the sticky throttle: origin offset by
the throttle). Full deflection stays `r = pad/2 - 24 px`. Tests compare the touch mapping with a
calibrated radio through `mapFrame` (same deflection -> same channels -> same setpoints) and show the
old absolute pad's jumps as the negative control. Pointer latency in the page is measured by the
browser bench (event timestamp -> channel); a real tablet's digitiser latency needs a real tablet.

## 4. Sound

`sound.volume` 40 -> 15 %: master gain (v/100)^1.5 x 0.7 goes 0.177 -> 0.041, 12.8 dB quieter (about
-28 dB from full scale): a background whine that tells throttle by ear without filling the room. A
pilot who never touched the slider gets the new default (A.6); one who set it keeps it. Quick mute: a
speaker button on the top bar (phones have no keyboard) next to the pause menu's item and Settings.
